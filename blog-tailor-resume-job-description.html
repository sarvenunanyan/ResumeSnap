How to Tailor Your Resume to a Job Description in 2025

7 min read · Updated May 2025

Sending the same resume to every job is one of the most common — and costly — mistakes in any job search. Tailored resumes get significantly more callbacks than generic ones. The reason is simple: ATS systems filter for specific keywords, and hiring managers notice when a candidate has clearly read the job description.

Here's exactly how to tailor your resume efficiently, without rewriting it from scratch every time.

Key insight: You don't need a completely different resume for every job — you need a strong base resume that you make targeted adjustments to. Most tailoring takes 10-15 minutes once you know what to look for.

Step 1: Analyse the Job Description

Before changing anything on your resume, read the job description carefully — at least twice. On the second read, highlight or note:

  • Required skills and qualifications — these are non-negotiable and must appear on your resume
  • Preferred skills — include these if you have them
  • Repeated words and phrases — frequency signals importance to ATS
  • The job title itself — consider mirroring it in your resume title
  • Company values or culture words — weave these naturally into your summary

Step 2: Match Your Job Title

The job title on your resume is one of the most heavily weighted fields in ATS systems. If the role is called "Digital Marketing Manager" and your resume says "Online Marketing Lead," you may not match the search.

Where accurate, align your current or most recent job title to the language used in the posting. You can add the original title in brackets if needed.

Job posting: "Senior Product Designer"

Generic resume: "UX Designer"
Tailored resume: "Senior Product Designer (UX & Visual Design)"

Step 3: Rewrite Your Professional Summary

Your summary is prime real estate — it's often the first thing a recruiter reads after glancing at your name and title. For each application, update your summary to reflect the specific requirements of that role.

Include the job title, 1-2 of the most important required skills, and your most relevant achievement. Keep it to 2-3 sentences.

"Senior Product Designer with 7 years of experience in SaaS product design and design systems. Led end-to-end design for products serving 2M+ users, with a focus on data-driven UX decisions and cross-functional collaboration."

Step 4: Add Missing Keywords to Skills

Compare the skills section of the job description with your skills section. Add any skills you genuinely have that aren't listed. The goal isn't keyword stuffing — it's ensuring your real competencies are described in the same language the employer uses.

Step 5: Reorder Your Experience Bullet Points

Within each role, lead with the bullet point most relevant to the job you're applying for. If you're applying for a leadership role, lead with leadership achievements. If it's a technical role, lead with technical accomplishments.

ATS and recruiters both read top-to-bottom — the first bullet point in each role gets the most attention.

Step 6: Adjust Quantified Achievements

If you have multiple quantified achievements to choose from, pick the ones that align best with what the employer cares about. A revenue-focused company wants to see revenue numbers. A growth-stage startup wants to see scale. A public sector role may prioritise efficiency and cost savings.

What Not to Change

  • Don't fabricate experience or skills you don't have
  • Don't change dates or misrepresent job titles
  • Don't over-optimise to the point where your resume reads unnaturally
  • Don't remove legitimate experience just because it isn't in the job description

The 15-Minute Tailoring Checklist

  • Update job title to match posting language
  • Rewrite professional summary with role-specific keywords
  • Add 2-3 missing keywords to skills section
  • Reorder bullet points in most recent role
  • Check company name is correct throughout
  • Run through Job Match Analyser to verify score
